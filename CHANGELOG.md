# Changelog

What changed in each Honeycomb release, newest first. New work goes under
**Unreleased**; `scripts/release.sh` turns that section into the next version
and uses it as the GitHub release notes.

## Unreleased

- Hearts reach Last.fm now: loving or unloving a song in Honeycomb loves or unloves it on Last.fm too (it waits and goes out later if you're offline). Settings → Plugins → Scrobbling has an **Import loved songs** button that hearts the songs you've loved on Last.fm that are in your library. It only goes one way on purpose: Last.fm doesn't remember unloves, so syncing back and forth would bring them back.
- Syncing to a device: playlists keep songs that are both on a server and in your own files (they used to drop out of the playlist file), replacing a file on a nearly full drive no longer runs out of space halfway, and folders left with only Finder or Windows leftovers (`.DS_Store`, `._…`, `Thumbs.db`) get cleaned up.
- Honeycomb has two faces now: **Listening** and **Advanced**. Listening is new, and it's for sitting back and playing music: a wall of album covers, your artists (as little honeycomb cells, of course), your playlists and every song, with search across all four. Click an album for its songs, hit Play or Shuffle, and click the song in the player bar for a big Now Playing view with the cover, lyrics that follow along, what's up next, stars and the heart. Right-click menus only have listening things in them (play, play next, queue, add to a playlist, love, go to the album or artist, keep a server album offline). Advanced is the Honeycomb you already know, with the column browser, the track table and all its columns, tag editing, the Inbox and smart playlist rules. Nothing was taken out of it. The Listening / Advanced switch sits in the sidebar (and at the top right in Listening), Cmd+L (Ctrl+L on Windows and Linux) flips it from the keyboard, and Honeycomb remembers where you left it. Both are the same library, the same queue and the same player, so switching never interrupts the music, and Sync and Settings are there in both. Songs credited to several artists ("A; B") show each one as their own link, and the album turns up on every one of their pages. Need to fix a tag while you're in Listening? Right-click → **Open in Advanced mode** takes you straight to that album. New here? The first-start setup asks which one you'd like to start in.
- Plugins run in their own sandbox now. Each one gets a little room of its own: it can see your library, make playlists, control playback and talk to the sites listed under **Access** in Settings → Plugins, and that's all. Scrobbling works just like before, and turning a plugin off now really unloads it instead of waiting for the next start. If you wrote a plugin yourself, it needs a small update for plugin API 2: [docs/plugins.md](docs/plugins.md) has the list.
- Honeycomb can play from your Navidrome now (or any other Subsonic / OpenSubsonic server)! Settings → Library → **Add a server…**, type in the address and your login, and its whole library shows up next to your own music: browse it, search it, put it in playlists and smart playlists like any other song. The music streams from the server and starts playing right away, and albums stay gapless even while streaming. Your plays, hearts, ratings and playlists go back to the server, so they're the same everywhere you listen. If you're offline for a bit, they wait and get sent once the server's back. If Honeycomb's scrobbling is on, adding a server reminds you to let only one of them scrobble, so your plays don't count twice on Last.fm or ListenBrainz.
- Right-click an album → **Keep album offline** and it downloads to your computer, so it plays without the server too (a little ● next to the title means it's here). Songs added to that album on the server later come along on their own. Offline songs sync to devices like your own files do. Honeycomb also checks your servers when it starts and every 15 minutes, quietly, so ratings, hearts, play counts and new music from other apps come in on their own. Album covers from the server are saved on your computer after each sync too, one per album, so the whole library keeps its art when you're offline.
- Server behind Cloudflare Access or another proxy that wants a client certificate? Pick your .p12 file when adding the server and Honeycomb shows it on every request, streams and covers included. The certificate stays on your computer and its password goes in the keychain. If you forget it, Honeycomb tells you it hit a sign-in page instead of just failing.
- Want your libraries kept apart? Once you have two music folders, or a folder and a server, a ⧉ button shows up next to **Library** in the sidebar (and at the top in Listening). It lists each music folder and each server: untick one and its songs leave Music, the browser, search and the Artists / Albums / Genres lists until you tick it again. Playlists and Next Up still show everything you put in them.
- There's a **Stream from servers** setting for when you're away from home: original files (best, and gapless) or Opus / MP3 at lower bitrates to save data. **Keep offline as** does the same for albums you keep offline: Opus or MP3 copies fit far more music on a laptop, and they play and sync to devices like any other offline song.
- **Disconnect** on a server's row takes its songs out of your library again. The music on the server isn't touched, and anything you kept offline can stay on your computer as normal files in Music/Honeycomb, ratings and play counts included.
- Tag editing and Show in folder are off for server songs, since the files live on the server. And ratings on them are whole stars, because that's all Subsonic servers keep.
- ReplayGain is here! Settings → Playback → ReplayGain levels out the volume between songs using the ReplayGain tags in your files, so a quiet old record and a loud new one stop fighting over your volume knob. **Track** plays every song at about the same loudness, **Album** keeps the quiet and loud songs of an album the way they were mixed. It switches on the exact first sample of the next song, so gapless albums stay gapless, and when the tags say how loud a song peaks, it won't turn it up so far that it clips. Your files aren't touched. The signal path (click the quality badge) shows how much it changed the current song, or says when a song has no tags. Songs without ReplayGain tags just play as they are; a ReplayGain scanner can add them. Honeycomb reads all your files once on the first scan after updating to pick up the tags.
- If a music folder can't be reached when Honeycomb starts (a drive that isn't plugged in, a network share that's offline), its songs now stay in your library with their ratings, play counts and playlist spots, instead of being treated as deleted. Adding or removing a folder while a scan is running also gets picked up now, and rating or hearting lots of songs at once is much quicker.
- Big libraries feel smoother: loading the library, editing tags on lots of songs and importing or exporting playlists no longer freeze the window, and changing the volume or pausing with a huge queue is instant.
- Before an update upgrades your library, Honeycomb now keeps a copy of it next to the original (library.db.v<number>.bak), and if the library ever can't be opened you get a message saying so instead of the app just closing.
- Plays count more honestly: a song that breaks off partway (a file that can't be read to the end, a stream that drops) isn't counted as played, and plays during a long scan on a slow drive aren't lost anymore.
- With a drive unplugged, playback stops after five songs that can't be found, instead of trying the whole queue one by one.
- Fewer Keychain prompts on the Mac. All of Honeycomb's logins (server passwords, certificate passwords, the Last.fm sign-in) now live in one Keychain entry that's read once per start, so macOS asks for access once instead of once per login. Logins saved by older versions move over by themselves the first time they're used.
- **Library health**, in Advanced mode's sidebar, checks your music files for what makes a library look messy: albums that show up as several (a compilation with no album artist, or "Band" vs "band"), missing or doubled track numbers, songs imported twice, albums with no cover, year or genre, and albums with gaps in their track numbers. Split albums can be fixed with one click, which gives every song the album's most common name, album artist and year; everything else is listed with its songs one click away, since those need you to decide.
- Honeycomb watches your music folders now. Drop an album into one (or let a download finish there) and it shows up in the Inbox a couple of seconds later, no Rescan now needed. Files removed or renamed outside Honeycomb are picked up the same way.
- A sync can run in the background now. Hide the Sync window and it carries on, with the ⇅ Sync button counting along ("Syncing 120 / 800"). Click it to bring the progress back or cancel, and a note pops up when it's done. Starting a second sync while one is running is refused, so two can't write to the same device at once.
- Server libraries are sturdier. If a server answers with no songs at all (it's busy rescanning, or its music drive isn't mounted), Honeycomb keeps your server songs and their playlist spots instead of taking them out. Plays, hearts and ratings waiting to go to a server are sent exactly once, even when a sync and the background sender run at the same time. A song the server refuses to send no longer stops the rest of your offline albums from downloading. And if the disk fills up while you disconnect a server and keep its offline copies, the songs that made it over stay as your files and the rest stay with the server, so you can just try again.
- Your server sees what Honeycomb is playing now: a server song shows up in Navidrome's Now Playing (and in anything that follows it) as soon as it starts, streamed, transcoded or offline. The play itself still counts once you've heard the song, like before.
- Server songs sync to devices and folders now, without keeping them offline first: Honeycomb downloads them straight into the sync folder in your **Keep offline as** quality (or as originals, converted on the way, when the sync converts). Pick a folder on this computer as a sync destination and **Update after server syncs** is ticked for it, so new music in the playlists, artists and albums you chose lands there on its own whenever Honeycomb checks your servers. When a server can't be reached, its songs play from those copies. And a sync folder inside one of your music folders no longer shows every synced song twice in your library: the scanner leaves out files a sync put there (existing doubles go away on the next scan).
- Adding a cover.jpg (or folder.png and the like) to an album folder now gives its songs a cover after the next scan, and the folder watcher starts that scan on its own. Before, it took a change to the songs themselves.

## 0.2.0, 2026-11-03

- Sync can convert now! In ⇅ Sync, pick what lossless files (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF) should become on the device: MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, or 16-bit FLAC for players that should stay lossless but are short on space. MP3s, AACs and Oggs you already have just get copied, since converting them again would only make them worse. Tags and cover art come along, albums stay gapless, and the encoders are built in, so there's nothing extra to install. AAC uses Apple's own encoder, so for now it's Mac-only. The first sync takes a while, but later ones only convert what's new or changed, and switching to another format swaps the files out instead of leaving two of each.
- Updates install themselves now! When the bulb lights up, click it and hit Install, and Honeycomb downloads the new version, checks that it really came from me (every update is signed) and restarts on it. No more going to GitHub and installing over the top by hand. This only works from this version on, so 0.1.1 still has to be updated the old way one last time.
- You can finally see what quality you're listening at. There's a little badge next to the song title in the player bar (like `FLAC 24/96` or `MP3 320`, gold for lossless), and clicking it shows the whole signal path: the file, whether it gets resampled or downmixed, the volume, and what the output device runs at. If something changes the audio on the way out, the badge gets a small orange dot.
- Honeycomb now knows the bit depth of lossless files. There's a Bit Depth column and a smart playlist rule for it, so "everything 24-bit" is one rule away. Lossless files get read again once on the first scan after updating to fill it in.
- The Inbox is a real waiting room now. New music that shows up in your folders sits in the Inbox until you say so, and stays out of the library, the column browser, smart playlists and sync until then. You can still play it from the Inbox to have a listen first. Right-click → **Add to Library** to keep it (dropping it on a playlist does the same), or **Remove and ignore** to get rid of it: the files stay where they are, but Honeycomb stops picking them up on rescans. Changed your mind? Settings → Library → **Ignored files** lists everything you ignored, and you can bring a whole bunch back at once (Select all, or Shift-click a range).
- Click an artist or album name in the song list to jump straight to it. And there's a Shuffle option when you right-click several songs, an artist or an album, so shuffling someone's whole discography is right-click → Shuffle (in the browser up top or the sidebar's Artists list).
- The Genre / Artist / Album browser up top got smarter. Shift-click picks a range, Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on Mac) adds or removes one, so you can look at five albums at once. And right-clicking there works like right-clicking songs, for everything in that row (or everything you picked): play it, put it on a playlist, rate it or edit the tags of a whole album in one go, and in the Inbox add or remove the lot.
- There's a setup screen now. New users get walked through adding their music folders, importing from another player, the listening history and the plugins that come with Honeycomb. If you already use Honeycomb, you'll see it once after updating, with just the new bits: whether to keep a listening history, and which plugins to turn on.
- Honeycomb now keeps a listening history: which songs you play, when, and how each one ended (played to the end, skipped, went back, stopped), down to the second you skipped at. It's the groundwork for playlists that learn what you actually like. It's off until you turn it on in Settings → Listening history (a first-start setup screen will ask about it later), it never leaves your computer, and you can clear it any time.
- Sync artists and albums, not just playlists. Pick a few artists or albums in the browser up top (or the sidebar's Artists or Albums list), right-click → **Sync to a device…**, and they're added to that device's list next to its playlists. They stay there: the next sync brings along new albums by those artists too. Untick one in the Sync window to take it off.
- Drag songs to reorder them in a playlist or in the queue. Grab one (or a few selected ones) and drop them where the line shows; the song that's playing keeps playing, wherever it ends up. It works whenever the list is in its own order, so if you sorted by a column, click the header until the sort is off first. Dragging songs onto a playlist in the sidebar works on Mac now too; it quietly never did before.
- Hearts! You can love songs now: click the ♥ in the song list (it shows up when you hover a row), right-click → **Love**, or hit the heart under the stars in Now Playing. Songs you loved in another player come over with the import, and smart playlists can already use them ("Loved is 1"). If you set up your columns before, you can add the ♥ column by right-clicking the column headers.
- Files Honeycomb can't read don't get read over and over on every rescan anymore, which makes rescans faster with a few broken files around. Settings → Library → **Unreadable files** lists them with the reason (like an empty file from a download that didn't finish), and Honeycomb tries a file again by itself as soon as it changes.
- Moving or renaming your music files inside a library folder doesn't lose their play counts, ratings or playlist spots anymore. Honeycomb notices it's the same song and just follows it to the new place.
- Sync is safer with drives someone else set up. If a folder on the drive is really a link to somewhere else on your computer, Honeycomb now refuses to write through it even on the very first sync, and a leftover half-copied file on the drive can't trick it into overwriting something else.

## 0.1.1, 2026-10-29

So this one is a big one. The two things I promised right after the alpha are here, scrobbling and syncing to players, plus a plugin system that scrobbling now runs on, and a whole lot of security work that happened before the repo went public.

### Scrobbling (Last.fm and ListenBrainz)

Scrobbling is a plugin that comes with Honeycomb, so it stays off until you want it. Go to Settings → Plugins, turn on Scrobbling and sign in right there. For Last.fm you sign in on Last.fm's own website, so Honeycomb never sees your password, and for ListenBrainz you paste the user token from your ListenBrainz settings page. A song counts once you've heard half of it or 4 minutes of it (only songs over 30 seconds, that's Last.fm's rule), and both services show what you're playing right now. If you're offline the scrobbles just wait and go out later, in the order you heard them and only once. Signing out of a service drops whatever was still waiting for it.

Why a plugin and not built in? Well, it keeps the core app smaller, and it's also the best real-life test the plugin system could get. Release builds come with Honeycomb's Last.fm API key, which gets added while the app is being built and is NOT in the repo. If you build Honeycomb yourself without one, the plugin lets you paste your own key instead.

### Sync to a player or flash drive

There's a new ⇅ Sync button at the bottom of the sidebar. Pick where to sync and which playlists (or the whole library), and Honeycomb copies over only what changed, writes each playlist as an .m3u8 the device can read, keeps file names safe for FAT32 and exFAT, and checks there's enough free space before it starts. It keeps a small manifest file on the device and only ever replaces or removes files it put there itself, so the rest of your stuff stays where it is. Cancelling or pulling the cable halfway through leaves no half-copied tracks behind.

And if you have a Rockbox player, turn on playback logging in its settings and the plays from the player get added to your play counts on the next sync. They don't get scrobbled yet though, because Rockbox saves the time in local time and I need to sort that out first.

### Plugins

Settings → Plugins is new. A plugin is a folder with a `plugin.json` and a JavaScript file that you drop into the plugins folder (there's a "Show plugins folder" button for that). Plugins can add things to the sidebar and to Settings, make playlists, play or queue tracks, follow what's playing, and match songs from other services to the ones in your library. They're off until you turn them on, they can only reach the websites their `plugin.json` lists, the logins they save go into your system keychain, and a browser sign-in can only come back on the ports the plugin lists.

One honest warning though: plugins run inside Honeycomb with the same access as the app itself, so those limits stop mistakes but they're not a sandbox (yet). Only turn on plugins you trust. How to write one is in `docs/plugins.md`.

### Security

The repo went public, so before that I went through the whole app looking for anything sketchy and fixed what turned up:

- Logins (Last.fm, ListenBrainz and anything a plugin saves) live in your system's keychain: Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, Secret Service on Linux. They're never in Honeycomb's database.
- The app window has a Content Security Policy, so it only runs scripts that Honeycomb itself serves.
- Tag edits get written to a copy first, and the copy only replaces the original once the write is complete, so a crash in the middle of saving can't wreck a file.
- Sync checks every path in the device's manifest and never touches anything outside the folder you synced to, even through symlinks. Before this fix a messed-up manifest on the device could point it somewhere else, which was the biggest thing the review found.
- Every answer from the internet (GitHub, LRCLIB, Last.fm, ListenBrainz and plugins) has a size limit, so a broken server can't make Honeycomb eat all your memory.
- The update check only ever offers download links that point to GitHub.
- Updated rustls-webpki and webpki-roots for RUSTSEC-2026-0382 and RUSTSEC-2026-0381.
- Every release comes with a `SHA256SUMS.txt` so you can check your download (how to do it is in `SECURITY.md`), the GitHub Actions that build it are pinned to exact versions, and the dependencies get checked with cargo audit.
- `SECURITY.md` says how to report a problem privately and lists everything Honeycomb connects to, and the privacy policy on honeycomb.n1ji.dev now covers scrobbling and sync too.

Honestly, I didn't even know about most of these until I ran a proper security check on the whole app, and I was kinda bamboozled by how many things it found. Some were tiny, like a setting that could have been stricter, and some were actually pretty bad, like the sync one above. But that's exactly why you check BEFORE a lot of people are using it and not after, so I'm glad they all got found now, and I'll keep running that check before every release from here on, and adding to this check more and more until there's literally nothing left for it to find (spoiler: that day is NEVER coming).

### Smaller stuff

The layout doesn't fall apart anymore when you make the window small. Nothing gets cut off at the right edge, the track list columns squeeze down instead of falling off the side, and Settings and Sync at the bottom of the sidebar stay on one line and in line with each other. The Rating column was also juuust too narrow for five stars with some fonts, so the last star turned into "…". Fixed that too.

Releases have real version numbers now (0.1.1, 0.1.2 and so on) and this changelog. Every build can also run a self-test that scans, decodes and actually plays test files through your speakers (`scripts/smoke-test.sh`), so a broken build shouldn't make it out the door.

That's it for this one. If something breaks, open an issue on GitHub and I'll take a look!

## 0.1.0 (alpha-1), 2026-10-22

The first alpha for macOS and Windows.

- Library with a Genre / Artist / Album column browser, fast search and sorting, tested with 100,000 tracks.
- Kaseki, Honeycomb's own playback engine: gapless playback, output at the file's own sample rate when the device allows it, surround folded down to stereo.
- Tag editor for one or many tracks, with album art.
- Manual and smart playlists, M3U import and export.
- Import play counts, ratings and playlists from an iTunes-format library XML and M3U files.
- Half-star ratings, Inbox for newly found files, synced lyrics (from the file, a .lrc next to it, or LRCLIB).
- Media keys and the system's Now Playing controls.
- Movable panels, a customizable player bar and track list columns, themes and colors.
- An update bulb in the sidebar that says when a newer release is out.
