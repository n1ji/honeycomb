Privacy policy

Honeycomb has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your library stays on your computer.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Who this is

Honeycomb is made by n1ji, an independent developer. Questions about this policy go to hello@n1ji.dev.

What stays on your computer

Everything Honeycomb knows about your music is stored in a database in the app’s data folder on your own computer: the folders you add, track details read from your files, ratings, play counts, playlists, lyrics it has found and your settings. None of it is uploaded anywhere, and I can’t see it.

Honeycomb reads your music files and only writes to them when you save changes in the tag editor. Libraries you import from another player are read on your computer too.

If you sync music to a player or flash drive, Honeycomb copies files to the drive you choose and leaves a small list of what it copied there. On a Rockbox player it also reads the player’s own log of what you played, to add those plays to your play counts. All of this happens between your computer and the drive.

To show the current song in your system’s media controls (the Now Playing widget on macOS, the media overlay on Windows), Honeycomb passes the title, artist, album and cover to your operating system. That stays on your device.

What goes online

Honeycomb connects to the internet for these things only. The last two stay off until you sign in.

  • Update check. When Honeycomb starts, then every 30 minutes, it asks GitHub’s API for the newest Honeycomb release and compares it with the build you’re running. The request includes your build’s commit ID and Honeycomb’s version number. A development build made from a git checkout runs git fetch on that checkout instead. GitHub sees your IP address, as with any request, and handles it under GitHub’s privacy statement. This check can’t be turned off yet. A setting for it is planned. Updates are only downloaded when you click Install: Honeycomb then fetches the new version from the release on GitHub and checks its signature before installing it.
  • Lyrics lookup. If a song has no lyrics in its tags or in a .lrc file next to it, Honeycomb asks LRCLIB, a free lyrics database, sending the song’s title, artist, album and length. LRCLIB sees your IP address. Answers are saved on your computer so the same song isn’t looked up again. You can turn this off in Settings with “Look up lyrics online”.
  • Last.fm scrobbling, only if you turn on the Scrobbling plugin under Settings → Plugins and sign in there. You sign in on Last.fm’s own website, so Honeycomb never sees your password. Last.fm gives Honeycomb a session key, which is stored in your system’s keychain (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, Secret Service on Linux), not in Honeycomb’s database. From then on, when a song starts Honeycomb tells Last.fm what’s playing now. Once you’ve heard half of a song longer than 30 seconds, or 4 minutes of it, it sends a scrobble. A scrobble contains the song’s artist, title, album, album artist, length and track number, and the time you started listening. The “now playing” message has only the artist, title, album and length. Last.fm keeps your scrobbles on your profile under Last.fm’s privacy policy.
  • ListenBrainz scrobbling, only if you turn on the Scrobbling plugin under Settings → Plugins and connect ListenBrainz there with the user token from your ListenBrainz settings. The token is stored in your system’s keychain (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, Secret Service on Linux), not in Honeycomb’s database. Honeycomb sends ListenBrainz “now playing” messages and listens with the song’s artist, title, album, length and track number, the time you started listening (for listens), and “Honeycomb” and its version number as the app that sent them. ListenBrainz is run by the MetaBrainz Foundation, and listens are public on your profile. See MetaBrainz’s privacy policy.

Scrobbles wait in the database on your computer until they’re sent, so songs played offline go out once you’re back online. Signing out of a service deletes its session key or token from your computer and drops any scrobbles still waiting for it. What the service already received stays on your account there.

Honeycomb doesn’t send crash reports, usage statistics or any identifier for you or your computer. The only exception is the Last.fm or ListenBrainz account you choose to sign in to.

This website

honeycomb.n1ji.dev uses no cookies, no analytics and no trackers. It runs on my own server behind Cloudflare, so Cloudflare and the server see the usual request details (your IP address, browser and the page you asked for) and may keep them in short-lived logs for security and troubleshooting. I don’t use them for anything else. The site loads its font from Google Fonts, so Google sees your IP address when the font is fetched.

Download links go to GitHub, which has its own privacy statement.

If you email me

If you write to hello@n1ji.dev or open an issue on GitHub, I’ll see what you send and use it only to reply and fix things. I don’t share it or add you to any list.

Children

Honeycomb doesn’t collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Changes

If a future version starts sending anything new, it will be off until you turn it on, and this page will say what it sends before it ships. The date at the top shows the last change.
